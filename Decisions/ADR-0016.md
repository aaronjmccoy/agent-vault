Given     Once a [[spec]] is approved, no [[rule]] in force says in what order the [[agent]] builds it, so a half-built change can sit untested and be [[hard-to-judge]]. This sets the build phase of a [[job]].
Decide    In what order is an approved [[spec]] built, which decides how much of a half-done [[job]] you can trust?

Where A:  While building a [[job]], the [[agent]] works one [[requirement]] at a time and makes all its [[scenario|scenarios]] pass before starting the next.
          → each finished [[requirement]] is tested and safe to stop at · slower than building everything at once
Where B:  While building a [[job]], the [[agent]] first writes the [[test-step|test step]] for every [[selector]] the [[spec]] uses, sees each [[scenario]] fail, then builds until all of them pass.
          → every [[scenario]] is shown to test something real · more work before any feature appears
Where C:  While building a [[job]], the [[agent]] builds the whole change, then runs every [[scenario]] once at the end.
          → fewest test runs · failures show late and tangled together
Combines  A and B can be picked together; C excludes both.
Recommend A and B: you can stop at any [[requirement]] and trust what is done, and no [[scenario]] passes by testing nothing.

Decision  A+B
