Given     In [[ADR-0012]] you said [[agent|agents]] should hand each other contracts. I read a contract as a [[message]] that fixes what must come back and how it is checked, so the receiving [[agent]]'s work is not [[hard-to-judge]].
Decide    What must a handoff [[message]] fix in advance?

Where A:  When an [[agent]] hands a [[job]] to another [[agent]], the [[agent]] shall write a [[message]] that names the [[job]]'s [[spec]], and the receiving [[agent]] shall answer with a [[message]] giving a [[result]] for each [[requirement]].
          → every handoff is checked against [[requirement|requirements]] · each handoff needs a [[spec]], even a small one
Where B:  When an [[agent]] hands a [[job]] to another [[agent]], the [[agent]] shall write a [[message]] stating what must be returned and how it will be checked, and the receiving [[agent]] shall answer with a [[message]] that meets it.
          → light contracts with no [[spec]] needed · nothing runs the check, so it is only as good as the wording
Where C:  When an [[agent]] hands a [[job]] with a [[spec]] to another [[agent]], the [[agent]] shall write a [[message]] that names that [[spec]], and otherwise the [[message]] shall state what must be returned and how it will be checked.
          → tested contracts where a [[spec]] exists, written ones elsewhere · two kinds of handoff to keep straight
Combines  exclusive
Recommend C: every handoff gets a contract, and only work that already has a [[spec]] pays for a tested one.
