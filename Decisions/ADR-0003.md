Given     Unclear intent hides inside undefined words, which makes a [[spec]] [[hard-to-judge]]. Linking every noun to a [[term]] note makes a missing definition visible, and a script can check for one.
Decide    Which words may a [[spec]] line use, and so which of them can you look up as a [[term]]?

Where A:  When writing a [[spec]] line, the author uses only [[ears|EARS]] and [[gherkin|Gherkin]] keywords, the fixed operators (is, equals, above, below, within, contains, not) and [[selector|selectors]], and links every other noun as a short [[term]] [[link]].
          → readable and checkable · undefined words show up as missing notes
Where B:  When writing a [[spec]] line, the author links every word, verbs included, by its full address.
          → completely uniform · hard to read and costly for [[agent|agents]]
Combines  exclusive
Recommend A: strict where it matters, readable everywhere else.

Decision  A
Consequences
- Where a word has a meaning of its own, the author gives it one [[term]] note, whose folder is its namespace, and qualifies a [[link]] only when two names collide.
- When writing a [[term]] note, the author uses SKOS-style frontmatter: notation (a stable id), a type, aliases, a version and a status, with a one-sentence definition; the type lives in the note, never in its address.
- When a [[term]]'s meaning changes, the author raises its version by one, and [[agent|agents]] use only the newest.
