Given     Most [[person|people]] ask an [[agent]] to "check your work" and expect it to know what that means. A check whose [[scenario|scenarios]] were never agreed is [[hard-to-judge]]: a pass says nothing about what was left out.
Decide    What must happen when a [[person]] asks for a check?

Where A:  When a [[person]] asks for a check, the [[agent]] first lists the [[requirement|requirements]] and [[scenario|scenarios]] it will test as a compact table and confirms them with the [[person]], and the [[verifier]] then checks the [[job]]'s change against them and writes a [[verdict]], with [[evidence]] for each [[result]] and at most five [[finding|findings]].
          → you see exactly what "checked" will cover before it runs, and a pass means what you agreed · one confirmation step per check
Where B:  When a [[person]] asks for a check, the [[agent]] first writes the [[requirement|requirements]] and [[scenario|scenarios]] it will test as a [[spec]] in the project's Specs folder and confirms it with the [[person]], and the [[verifier]] then checks the [[job]]'s change against it and writes a [[verdict]], with [[evidence]] for each [[result]] and at most five [[finding|findings]].
          → the agreed tests are kept and can be run again later · slower to set up than a table, even for a small check
Combines  exclusive
Recommend A: a check starts by agreeing what will be tested, so a pass covers everything agreed.

Decision  A

Consequences
- When a [[person]] asks for a check, the [[agent]] shows the test list in its [[response|reply]] and asks you to Approve it in the Needs you row, then checks once you approve ([[Draft-0049]]).
- When the [[verifier]] writes a [[verdict]], it saves it as a YAML record next to the [[spec]], and its [[response|reply]] gives a [[link]] to it in the Check row ([[Draft-0050]]).
- When a [[verdict]] is written, each [[scenario]] gets a [[result]], and a [[requirement]] passes only when all its [[scenario|scenarios]] pass ([[Draft-0051]]).
