Given     No [[rule]] in force says when a [[job]]'s [[spec]] is written or who approves it, so an [[agent]] may build first and leave you a change that is [[hard-to-judge]]. This sets where planning sits in a [[job]].
Decide    Do you approve a [[job]]'s [[spec]] before any code is changed?

Where A:  When a [[person]] makes a request, the [[agent]] drafts the [[job]]'s [[spec]] and asks the [[person]] to approve it before changing any code.
          → you always see what will be built first · one extra approval on every [[job]], even a one-line fix
Where B:  Where a [[job]] changes more than one file, the [[agent]] drafts its [[spec]] and asks the [[person]] to approve it before changing any code; otherwise it builds and writes the [[spec]] alongside the change.
          → small fixes go straight through · the [[agent]] judges size, and a wrong guess skips your approval
Where C:  When a [[person]] makes a request, the [[agent]] writes the [[spec]] and builds the change at once, and the [[person]] reviews both together at the check.
          → fastest start · a misread request is found only after it is built
Combines  exclusive
Recommend B: it puts your approval where a wrong build costs most, without slowing small fixes.

Decision  A
